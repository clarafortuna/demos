This process works today because experienced people fill the gaps a system should cover. Here are the six biggest exposures, each paired with the control the new system needs to provide. The facts reflect procedures 4022 and 9980; the risk ratings are our analysis.
High Risk

The Spreadsheet Is the System of Record

Today: every case lives in an Excel file passed around by email.
Exposure: versions conflict, attachments get lost, and no one has a single view of open cases.
Move each case into a single live record in Dataverse, with email used only for notifications.
High Risk

Safety Timers Run on Memory

Today: the one‑hour wait and the two‑hour police follow‑up depend on someone watching the clock.
Exposure: during a storm, a missed deadline can easily go unnoticed.
Build in automatic timers, reminders, and a dashboard for every open case.
High Risk

Shift Handoff of Open Welfare Checks

Today: handoffs rely on a personal briefing and an email copy, with no link to the shift roster.
Exposure: an open welfare check could sit without an owner overnight.
Give every case an owner at all times and route open work to whoever is on shift.
High Risk

Jurisdiction Routing and Recorded Calls

Today: finding the right Westchester municipality is a manual lookup, and call recording depends on remembering to click Snip.
Exposure: calling the wrong department delays the welfare check, and a missed recording breaks compliance.
Look up the municipality automatically from the address and prompt for the recorded call.
Medium Risk

Duplicated Audit Trail

Today: the same information is typed three times, into CC&B, the worksheet, and the email.
Exposure: details drift between copies and notes get missed.
Capture the details once and generate the note and worksheet from them.
Medium Risk

Key-Person Bottlenecks

Today: CCG access runs through a handful of named people, and workload limits rest on judgment.
Exposure: a few individuals become single points of failure, and limits slip during busy events.
Map X‑access to security roles and enforce workload limits at assignment.
These Are Safety Issues, Not Efficiency Issues A dropped handoff or a missed timer puts a life‑support customer at risk, so the new system should treat these controls as mandatory.
Open Questions for the SMEs The procedures don't define what counts as a valid attempt, when the two‑hour clock starts, how the right municipality is confirmed, or how a handoff is accepted. The new system will need answers.
