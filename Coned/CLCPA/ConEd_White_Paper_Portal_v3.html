1. Project / Program Summary

Work Description. Consolidated Edison Company of New York ("CECONY" or "Company") proposes to design and implement an Affordable Multifamily Electrification Pilot ("Affordability Pilot" or "Pilot") that will provide bill credits to residents in multifamily naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) and low-income cooperative buildings to mitigate the cost shift that occurs when their buildings move from centralized fossil heating to in-unit heat pump systems tied to tenant meters.

The Pilot will allow the Company to collect new information about Pilot participants to produce an analysis of the heating cost shift, and to provide important learnings for a long-term policy solution.

2. Challenge Addressed

Multifamily buildings in New York City typically use centralized fossil fuel systems to provide heating. The building owner pays for fuel because there is usually one meter for the building; consequently, the building typically includes heating costs in residents' rent or through a common charge.

To achieve State and City building decarbonization goals, multifamily buildings will need to replace centralized fossil systems with electrified heating alternatives. A popular clean heat technology is a ductless heat pump, which relies on electricity for both space heating and cooling.

3. DAC & Sustainability Profile

Per CLCPA §7(3), this Pilot is partially located in three Bronx DAC census tracts (36005014300, 36005014500, 36005014700). Of the projected 4,820 customers served, 3,760 (78%) reside in DACs. Forecast benefits include sustained reductions in indoor and outdoor co-pollutant exposure, weatherization improvements, and direct affordability protection through the bill-credit mechanism. See Part 105 worksheet, rows 14–19, for the full burden / benefit assessment.
