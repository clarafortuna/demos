 5. THE CANDIDATE FIX, SIZED BEFORE IT IS BUILT
=======================================================================
  The principle is the one already ruled for the CLCPA-320 marker this
  session: DERIVABILITY COMES FROM THE RULE REGISTRY, not from row role,
  and by the same reasoning not from the value in the cell. Applied here,
  a total cell would be the engine's only where the engine has a
  DECLARED rule that produces it -- a derived column, a declared derived
  row, or a detectSumColumns relationship -- and not merely because the
  figure stored in it today happens to add up.

  This is NOT built. What follows is its blast radius, measured across
  every table and every stored year, so the size of the change is known
  before anyone decides to make it.

  total cells the importer refuses as rebuildable today : 445
  of those, cells with NO declared rule behind them     : 275
  tables affected: 24  ["A1","A2","A3","A4","A5","A6","A7","A8","B1","C2","C3","C4","C5","E1","F2","F7","F8","F9","G10","H1","J3","J4","J6","J7"]
      A1: 6 cells
      A2: 5 cells
      A3: 4 cells
      A4: 8 cells
      A5: 60 cells
      A6: 36 cells
      A7: 8 cells
      A8: 50 cells

  Every one of those cells would move from REFUSED to accepted and
  named. That is the direction CLCPA-272 ruled -- a provided value is
  accepted and reconciled, never rejected -- so the change is not
  dangerous in kind. It is large in degree, and it is a change to what
  the importer does on every table, which is why it is measured and
  left for a ruling rather than taken on my own judgement.

--- what this shows ---------------------------------------------------
  THE DISCARD REPRODUCES. On the reconstructed anatomy the file stages 40,
  14 land and 26 do not; on the anatomy whose grand total does not
  reconcile, 24 do not. The difference is exactly two cells, and they
  are exactly the two cells of the filed grand total, which is the
  hosted finding: the two missing were the two filed totals.

  Both are DISCARDED IN SILENCE: totals accepted and named is 0.
  Round 2 measured this same behaviour and called it correct, on the
  reasoning that the engine can reproduce the figure so the cell is the
  engine's. The provenance is what breaks that: the engine CANNOT
  derive r25 -- section 1 shows it writing null -- so the figure it
  reproduces is one an earlier FILE put there. The engine is claiming a
  cell on the strength of a number it did not write.

  The reason it is not correct is in section 2. The SAME two cells of
  the SAME table belong to the preparer or to the engine depending on
  whether the figures stored in them happen to add up: on the 2025
  anatomy r25 is not confirmed and its filed figure is kept and named,
  and on the reconstructed one it is confirmed and the filed figure is
  dropped in silence. Ownership of a cell is being decided by the value
  currently sitting in it, which is the classifier trap this engine has
  now ruled against three times: CLCPA-293s own comment, CLCPA-319s
  columnTotal, and the CLCPA-320 marker ruling that derivability comes
  from the rule registry and not from row role.
